How Fast Is The World Electronically?

Median Internet Speed by Country - Fixed Broadband (2024)

The choropleth map above illustrates the global disparities in fixed broadband internet speeds as of May 2024. Countries such as Singapore, Hong Kong, Chile, the United Arab Emirates and Iceland display the highest median internet speeds, reflecting advanced digital infrastructure. In contrast, majority of Africa display lighter shades, indicating slower speeds likely due to limited broadband penetration and outdated network technology.

Does Higher Population Mean Higher Speeds?

Mobile Median Speed (Mbps) vs Population (2024)

The chart above displaying a nation's population and its average mobile speed tells us that there is no clear relationship between a country's population size and its mobile internet speed. Nations with smaller populations, such as Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, and Singapore achieve the highest median mobile speeds, whereas highly populated countries such as India, Indonesia, and Nigeria tend to cluster at lower speed levels. This suggests that there is no connection between a nation's population size and its mobile speed.

The Top 5 Breakdown Comparison To Australia

In Figure 1, Singapore tops the world in broadband performance with a median speed of 290 Mbps, closely followed by Hong Kong (282.5 Mbps), Chile (274.5 Mbps), United Arab Emirates (272.9 Mbps), and Iceland (255.7 Mbps). These nations share strong fibre infrastructure, competitive telecom markets, and government-backed digital strategies that support high-speed connectivity.

In contrast, Australia's median speed of 69.4 Mbps remains considerably lower, underscoring the country's slower network upgrades and rural broadband limitations compared to global front-runners.

In Figure 2, Qatar leads global mobile performance with a median speed of 329.4 Mbps, followed closely by the United Arab Emirates (309.8 Mbps), Kuwait (233.8 Mbps), Denmark (147.3 Mbps), and Norway (142.2 Mbps). Each of these nations benefits from early 5G rollout, dense network coverage, and heavy investment in mobile infrastructure.

In comparison, Australia's median mobile speed of 93.4 Mbps lags well behind, highlighting slower 5G expansion and regional performance disparities across the country.

Costs Related To Electricity, Broadband And Mobile Costs

Electricity prices vary dramatically worldwide, with Bermuda, Italy, and the Ireland among the highest, exceeding $0.45 USD per kWh. In contrast, countries such as Cuba, Ethiopia and Iran have the lowest rates.

Fixed broadband costs are the highest in regions such as Solomon Islands, Burundi and Turks and Caicos Islands, reaching its highest above $400 USD per month. Conversely, nations with mature digital economies, like Malta, South Korea and Taiwan maintain far more affordable broadband access below $30 USD per month.

Mobile data pricing shows a steep global divide as Malawi, Benin and Chad record costs of above $25 USD per GB, whereas countries such as India, Italy and Denmark offer some of the world's cheapest mobile data, averaging under $1.0 USD per GB. This gap underscores the importance of competition and infrastructure scale in reducing consumer data costs.

How Does Australia Match Up Against The World

Australia's internet landscape remains moderate in cost but underwhelming in performance when compared to global leaders. While consumer broadband prices sit near the middle range globally, the average fixed broadband speed is significantly lower than countries such as Singapore, Hong Kong, and the United Arab Emirates.

Despite substantial investments in the NBN and 5G infrastructure, Australia's vast geography and lower population density continue to challenge service equity, especially across rural regions. This imbalance between affordability and speed highlights the need for ongoing investment to keep pace with global digital standards.
